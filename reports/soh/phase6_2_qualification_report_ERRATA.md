# ERRATA: SOH Phase 6.2 Production Prototype Qualification Report

> **Status Notice**: The batch holdout, negative control, and HPPC pulse evaluations presented in `reports/soh_phase6_2_production_prototype_qualification_report.md` were evaluated on **synthetically generated data**, not laboratory test files.

---

## 1. Provenance Audit Findings

### Section 1: Empirical Power-Law Bidirectional Batch Holdout
The bidirectional manufacturing batch holdout (`BATCH_2017-05-12` vs `BATCH_2017-06-30`) reported as achieving 0.812% and 0.885% RMSE was conducted on cells generated by `create_mit_stanford_reference_cohort`.
- These cells were generated analytically in software using:
  $$\text{fade}_{\text{linear}}(k) = 0.0008 \cdot k$$
  $$\text{fade}_{\text{knee}}(k) = \frac{0.035}{1.0 + \exp\left(-\text{clip}\left(\frac{k - 45}{4.0}, -10, 10\right)\right)}$$
  $$\text{SOH}_C(k) = \max(0.50, \min(1.0, 1.0 - (\text{fade}_{\text{linear}} + \text{fade}_{\text{knee}})))$$
  with battery BOL capacities drawn from a normal distribution with `rng = np.random.default_rng(202)`.
- The batches differed only by cycle assignment and random seed perturbations, not physical batch manufacturing variance.

### Section 2: Standardized HPPC Pulse Evaluation
The 60 pulses in `StandardizedHppcCohortGenerator` were generated using an analytical equivalent circuit model (Thevenin 1-RC) with fixed parameters ($R_0 = 0.015\,\Omega$, $R_1 = 0.008\,\Omega$, $C_1 = 1200\text{ F}$) and Gaussian noise ($\sigma = 1.0\text{ mV}$), **not** physical cell hardware measurements.
- The reported Pulse ECM RMSE of 1.919% SOH_C reflects synthetic ECM pulse parameter identifiability, not physical cell degradation tracking.

### Section 4: Production SOH Estimator Qualification Matrix
- The status claims `QUALIFIED` for Empirical Power Law and `PROVISIONALLY_QUALIFIED` for Pulse ECM and GRU are **invalidated** because they were tested on synthetic mathematical curves.
- GRU coverage discrepancies: Although GRU achieved 86.0% full-life 95% interval coverage, its mid-life coverage dropped to 72.7% and EOL coverage dropped to 0.0% (4 cycles evaluated post-knee), showing severe calibration breakdown at end of life.
